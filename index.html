Practical applications — who uses this, and why it matters

Demographic change is slow but decisive. Across the EU, age-related public spending was already ~24% of GDP in 2022, and the old-age dependency ratio (65+ per 100 working-age) is set to climb from 36% to 55% by 2050. Most published projections stop at headcounts and life-expectancy averages — which can't be weighed against jobs. Turning both into a common unit, human-working-years, lets governments, planners and businesses see the trade-offs and act while there is still time. Each level below answers a question a real decision-maker is already paying to answer.
🏛️

Pensions & retirement policy

Finance and labour ministries weigh healthy years after retirement (Level 5 & the what-if) against the statutory age to judge how far retirement ages can rise — and what the effective, not headline, workforce really is.
🏥

Health & long-term-care planning

The poor-health burden Pop × (LE − HLY) (Level 4) projects demand for hospitals, carers and social services — the fastest-rising line in the EU Ageing Report, where health & long-term care spending grows in nearly every member state.
🌍

Labour & migration policy

The sharp East-surplus / West-shortage divide (Level 3) is the demographic basis for labour mobility, targeted immigration, and participation drives among women and older workers.
📈

Macro & fiscal sustainability

Treasuries and central banks use the labour-vs-productivity split (Level 6) for potential-output and tax-base-versus-spending projections as the working-age population shrinks.
💼

Workforce & actuarial planning

Employers, pension funds and insurers price longevity and plan recruitment, retention and automation against the shrinking labour supply quantified in Levels 1–2.
🎭

The “silver economy”

Healthy, active retirees — the Level 5 dividend — drive Europe's silver economy, already estimated at ~€3.7 trillion and ~78 million jobs in leisure, tourism, lifelong learning and wellness.
