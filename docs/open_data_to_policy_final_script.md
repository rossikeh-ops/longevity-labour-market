# Speaker script — *From Open Data to Evidence-Based Policy*
### A Digital Dashboard for Monitoring Healthy Longevity, Business Dynamics, and Labour Market Sustainability
**Rositsa Nikolova** · PhD Researcher, Department of Management, UNWE
XIX International & Interdisciplinary Scientific Conference — *Vanguard Scientific Instruments in Management ’2026*

A first-person spoken script for the **10-slide final deck**. Runs **~12–14 minutes**

## Slide 1 · Title  *(0:40)*
*On screen: title slide.*

Good afternoon, and thank you to the organisers of the nineteenth *Vanguard
Scientific Instruments in Management* conference for the opportunity to present. My
name is **Rositsa Nikolova**; I am a PhD researcher in the Department of Management
at the University of National and World Economy. Today I want to talk about how we
can move **from open data to evidence-based policy** — through a digital dashboard
for monitoring **healthy longevity, business dynamics, and the sustainability of the
labour market**. Let me begin with why this question matters to me personally.

## Slide 2 · From medical technology to longevity systems  *(1:15)*
*On screen: "8 years in medical devices".*

For eight years I worked in the private healthcare sector — as a marketing manager
in a wholesale and retail company dealing in **CPAP devices, hearing solutions, and
home medical equipment**. That role gave me something a textbook cannot: direct,
everyday exposure to **chronic, insidious, age-related impairments** — the conditions
that slowly erode a person's cognitive capacity, their autonomy, and, crucially,
their **employability**. But I also saw the other side of it. Again and again, I
witnessed how **functional, assistive technologies preserve quality of life** — how
the right device, at the right time, keeps someone independent, active, and able to
keep contributing. That contrast — decline on one hand, and technology that holds it
back on the other — is what moved me from medical technology toward the study of
**longevity as a system**.

---

## Slide 3 · The mutual effect — two vectors  *(1:45)*
*On screen: Vector A / Vector B, dissertation topic.*

That question became my dissertation: **the mutual effect of longevity and business,
and its connection with the labour market**. And I want to stress that this effect
runs in **two directions**.

**Vector A — longevity transforming business and labour.** First, *workforce
longevity*: preserving functional health lets organisations retain skilled talent
well past traditional retirement ages. Second, the *silver and longevity economy*:
an expanding market for preventive health technology, diagnostics, non-invasive
therapies, and wellness services. And third, *workplace adaptation*: a restructuring
of organisations toward ergonomics, cognitive support, and flexible career pathways.

**Vector B runs the other way — business and labour driving longevity.** First,
*corporate health capital*: employer-sponsored screenings and assistive technology
directly reduce premature disability. Second, *commercialising research*: it is
private capital and scalable business models that translate scientific breakthroughs
into widely accessible medical products. And third, the *socioeconomic gradient*:
sustained, meaningful employment is itself a key social determinant of cognitive
resilience and life satisfaction.

So it is a genuine loop. And the point where these two vectors meet — where longevity
and business come together — **is the labour market**.

---

## Slide 4 · The challenge  *(1:00)*
*On screen: the cascade (Longevity → Participation → Sustainability).*

This leads to the framing of my whole talk. **Ageing is not only a health question —
it is an economic and labour-market question**, and it is placing unprecedented
pressure on our labour markets. You can picture it as a cascade. **Longevity science
and healthspan** flow into **labour-market participation** — who can work, and for
how long. Participation, in turn, flows into **economic and fiscal sustainability** —
whether our pension and health systems remain viable. Break the first link, and the
whole chain is under strain. So if we want to protect the bottom of that cascade, we
have to be able to **measure and manage the top**.

---

## Slide 5 · Labour-market equilibrium  *(1:20)*
*On screen: Balance = Supply − Demand, the driver lists.*

To measure it, we need a common language. The dashboard rests on a simple identity:
the **labour-market balance equals supply minus demand** — both expressed in one unit,
**human-working-years**. On the **supply** side, the drivers are life expectancy, the
expected duration of working life, the healthy working-age population, and — critically
— the **share of healthy life years**, the healthspan. On the **demand** side, the
drivers are employment rates, job-vacancy rates modelled through the Beveridge curve,
the required length of service, and the economic structure — the mix of sectors. The
strength of this framework is that it puts **health and jobs into the same equation**.
Extend healthspan, and you shift the supply side; change the economy, and you shift
demand. The balance between them *is* the labour market.

---

## Slide 6 · Where this dashboard sits  *(1:10)*
*On screen: the landscape of existing dashboards + the gap.*

Now — where does a dashboard like this sit? Because we are certainly not the first to
look at ageing and labour. There is a whole landscape of tools: the OECD's **Dashboard
on Older Workers**, **Eurostat's Ageing Europe**, the ILO's **ILOSTAT** explorer, the
**UNECE Active Ageing Index**, and national dashboards in the UK, Canada and the US.
They are valuable. But look at what they actually track: employment at 55-to-64 and
65-plus, participation by age band, effective versus statutory retirement, dependency
ratios, pension sustainability. In other words, they monitor **chronological age and
headcount**. What they largely leave out is **functional capacity — healthspan — and
the longevity dividend**. And that gap is exactly where I believe the future lies.

---

## Slide 7 · A paradigm shift  *(1:15)*
*On screen: the traditional vs healthy-longevity comparison table.*

That gap defines a **paradigm shift — from chronological age to functional capacity**.
Let me contrast the two. A traditional ageing-and-labour dashboard uses chronological
age — those arbitrary cut-offs, fifty-plus, sixty-five-plus. Its problem statement is
falling supply and pension pressure; it measures exit by statutory versus effective
retirement; it sees a static end-of-career cohort; and its interventions are things
like raising the retirement age. A **healthy-longevity** dashboard reframes every one
of those. The lens becomes **functional capacity — healthspan versus lifespan**. The
problem becomes the **mismatch between people's capacity and how our workplaces are
designed**. Exit is measured against **healthy, disability-free years**. The workforce
becomes a **dynamic life-course**, one where people reskill and adapt. And the
interventions shift to prevention, ergonomics, and phased retirement. Our dashboard
already takes that healthspan lens — because it measures **healthy life years, not
just headcount**.

---

## Slide 8 · The headline finding  *(1:10)*
*On screen: 89M aggregate · +369M East · −280M West.*

So what does the dashboard actually show? The headline finding is this. By **2033**,
across the eight countries in our study, the aggregate labour balance is **nearly
balanced** — a net of about **89 million** human-working-years. On its own, that number
looks reassuring. But it **masks a sharp geographic divide**. The **East** — Poland,
Romania, Czechia, Bulgaria — runs a surplus of about **plus 369 million**. The **West
and EFTA** — Germany, France, Switzerland, Norway — run a shortage of about **minus 280
million**. Two opposite imbalances that happen to cancel on paper. And that single
contrast is, in effect, the demographic engine behind **migration within Europe**. The
aggregate hides the story; the dashboard reveals it.

---

## Slide 9 · Conclusion  *(1:00)*
*On screen: "measurable, comparable, and actionable".*

To conclude. **Healthy longevity is measurable, comparable, and actionable.** Open data
and a transparent dashboard turn ageing from an abstract concern into **evidence that
policy can actually use** — and they reveal that the mutual effect of longevity and
business runs **straight through the labour market**. My own aim, drawing on both my
private-sector experience and my academic research, is to help move healthcare from a
model built around **treating disease** toward one built around **prevention and
extending healthspan** — bridging business and academia, science and policy. Because if
we manage healthspan well, longer lives need not be a burden. They can be a **dividend**.
