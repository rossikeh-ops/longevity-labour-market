Level 6 — The longevity dividend in the macroeconomy

Open horizons · how longevity and the labour force feed real GDP — and whether healthy longevity lifts productivity · 8 countries · observed → growth-accounted forecast to 2033.

📋 Data behind this report ↓
Real GDP = Employment × Productivity  ⟹  growth = labour + productivity the labour channel is the one the longevity / supply engine moves
Longevity reaches GDP through labour, not a productivity boost. Combined real GDP rises €8.0T → €8.6T by 2033 (≈ 0.8%/yr). Decomposed, the East (BG, PL, CZ, RO) grows productivity-led (catch-up convergence) with a flat-to-shrinking labour channel, while the West/EFTA leans more on labour. And the reverse arrow is absent: across countries, self-reported healthy share and productivity correlate -0.87 (the wrong sign — a GALI artifact), and within a country the link is nil (slope -0.07, R² 0.003).

What drives projected growth: labour vs productivity (2024 → 2033)

Each bar splits projected real-GDP growth (CAGR) into the productivity channel and the labour channel (employment — where demography and the longevity dividend act). The number is total GDP growth. A negative labour segment (e.g. Romania) is a demographic drag the productivity channel must offset.

Productivity (GDP per worker) Labour (employment)
How to read it. Productivity dominates in the East (rapid catch-up), so even shrinking workforces still grow. In the West, productivity growth is slow, so the labour channel matters more — which is exactly where raising participation, healthy working life and migration (the supply levers of Levels 1–3) have macro leverage. Growth-accounting is descriptive: it apportions, it does not prove causation.

Does healthy longevity raise productivity? (descriptive — no causal claim)

The reverse arrow (Direction 3): if longer healthy lives make a more productive workforce, healthy share (HLY/LE) and GDP-per-worker should move together. They don't.

Cross-country (2024)
The fitted line slopes the wrong way (corr -0.87): the most productive countries (CH, NO) report the lowest healthy share, the least productive (BG) the highest — self-perceived health (GALI) reflects expectations, not output.
Within-country, year-on-year
A shapeless cloud: changes in healthy share explain none of the change in productivity (slope -0.07, R² 0.003, n=149). Productivity grows ~1.6%/yr on capital, technology and convergence — not the health measure.
The capstone, honestly. The longevity dividend is real but it acts on the labour quantity — more healthy people working longer (Levels 1–3) — not on a measurable per-worker productivity premium. Where the workforce shrinks (Romania, Bulgaria), productivity convergence still carries growth for now; where convergence is exhausted (the West), the labour channel — and therefore the supply levers — is what moves the needle.
Honest limitations. • Growth-accounting is an identity decomposition, not a structural/causal model (no separate capital or TFP term — productivity bundles capital deepening and technology).
• Healthy share uses self-perceived HLY (GALI), which is not internationally comparable in levels — hence the perverse cross-country sign.
• GDP and productivity are forecast with the same simple ensemble (no neural nets); the productivity trend is the larger swing factor at 9 years.
