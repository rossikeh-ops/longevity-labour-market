Labour Supply — a guided story

8 European countries · to 2033
Step 1 · Demographics

The demographic baseline

Every projection starts from who is alive today. The pyramid shows the working-age and older cohorts by sex; drag the year to watch the bulge move up as Europe ages.

Year
Population aged 15+ by 5-year band (Eurostat demo_pjangroup). The share aged 65+ is the ageing signal that erodes the working-age base over time.
Step 2 · Participation

Labour-force participation: the closing gap

A shrinking population can still supply more labour if a larger share participates. The male–female participation gap has narrowed steadily — the lever the West uses to offset demographics.

Participation = active (employed + unemployed) ÷ working-age population, by sex (Eurostat lfsa_egan, une_rt_a).
Step 3 · Migration

Migration adjusts the baseline

Net migration is the biggest swing factor on the working-age population. We carry Eurostat's three official variants and forecast within that envelope.

Working-age (15–64) population under baseline, high- and low-migration variants (Eurostat projection proj_23np), calibrated to observed 2024.
Step 4 · Total supply

The synthesised supply forecast

Demographics × participation × healthy working life, combined into potential labour supply in human-working-years, observed then forecast to 2033 with an 80% uncertainty band.

Supply = healthy working-age people × expected working life. The longevity dividend (rising healthy-life share + longer careers) offsets the shrinking base.
