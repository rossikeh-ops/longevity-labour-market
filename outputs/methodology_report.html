Methodology — how the model works

A transparent ensemble of simple statistical models + Monte-Carlo — deliberately no neural networks (short series, defensibility required). Three diagrams describe the whole model.

The pipeline — Levels 0 → 3

Where each step sits and what it produces. Levels 1–2 run every driver through the ensemble engine (§1), then combine via the identities below; Level 3 subtracts the two.

Subtract the two stocks, by country × sex, to 2035 with Monte-Carlo uncertainty bands; surfaces the East-surplus / West-shortage divide.

Balance = Supply − Demand  →  human-working-years

1Ensemble forecast engine

For each time series (for every country × sex, over its full history) we do not rely on a single model — we average 4 simple models. Damped Holt and the naive anchor guard against over-extrapolation: socio-economic rates saturate, they do not grow linearly forever.

The uncertainty band (the amber area in the reports) is assembled from three sources, combined in quadrature: (1) the disagreement between the four models; (2) the real out-of-sample error from backtesting — we re-forecast history and measure how far off we are; and (3) the demographic uncertainty of the population. That is why the bands are a realistic 12–43% wide, not a falsely narrow ±5%.

On the supply side, the engine forecasts life expectancy, the healthy-life share (HLY/LE), working-life duration and the working-age population to 2035, which are then combined into healthy person-years.

Supply = healthy working-age people × expected working life person-years

2Building labour demand

From the forecast components we build demand using the formula from the case brief. Population is not extrapolated naively — we take Eurostat's official projection proj_23np, calibrated to the observed 2024 value.

3Building labour supply

The same logic as demand, but with two health–demographic multipliers. Both multipliers — the healthy-years share (HLY ÷ LE) and expected working life — are forecast with the same 4-model ensemble from the first diagram. The result is in person-years, exactly like demand — which is why the two are directly comparable.
