Whether retirement is healthy is not guaranteed: it ranges from +5.6 years in Bulgaria to -6.6 years in Switzerland — in several countries self-reported health declines before the retirement age. The aggregate dividend grows (199→438M person-years) as healthy-life years rise. But tested against leisure/education/culture spending, the link is statistically negligible within a country — consumption tracks income.

How to read it. The dividend = population × the positive healthy years beyond retirement. It grows quickly where rising HLY pushes more of the population across the retirement threshold — so it is sensitive to the HLY forecast (a self-perceived measure). Countries currently below zero (DE, CH, CZ) contribute little until their healthy-life years catch up to the retirement age.

Does the dividend drive consumption? (descriptive — no causal claim)

We relate it to leisure, education & culture spending (COICOP CP09 recreation, CP10 education, CP11 restaurants/hotels). Across countries they co-move — but within a country over time, they don't.

Within-country, year-on-year
A shapeless cloud: changes in the dividend explain none of the change in spending (within-diff elasticity -0.008, correlation -0.068).
The honest reading
Cross-country the fit looks strong (FE elasticity 0.038, R² 0.984), but that is mostly country size. Leisure/education/culture consumption grows ~2.2%/yr with household income and prices, statistically unrelated to the healthy-retirement dividend in the short run. So the dividend is a meaningful demographic quantity, but not a usable predictor of consumption — we report it descriptively.
Honest limitations. • HLY − retirement age uses self-perceived HLY (GALI), so the sign (healthy retirement or not) is partly subjective — Switzerland's −6.6y largely reflects low self-reported health.
• The dividend forecast is sensitive to HLY crossing the retirement threshold (clipped at zero).
• The consumption link is descriptive, not causal; the demographic dividend does not predict spending, which tracks income.
